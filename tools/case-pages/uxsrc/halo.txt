# 02 / The problem || Asking for help can be its own obstacle.
p: Every safety app I audited, from bSafe to Noonlight to the SOS built into the phone, treats the phone as the trigger. So the phone has to be reachable, unlocked and usable at the exact moment all three are hardest.
diagram: d-obstacles | Four things an SOS button depends on, each removed by a commute: a free hand, a visible screen, a working signal and time to think, with how HalO replaces each one
small: Each commute removes at least one thing an SOS button depends on. The green lines show how HalO replaces each one.
quote: How might we :: How might we get someone from fear to help when the screen is not available?
p: The women I designed for already take precautions. The gap is the moment itself. It had to work in a city and a village, under stress without looking down, and be discreet enough to wear every day.

# 05 / Users || Two commuters with the same need and almost opposite constraints.
p: Both want someone to help them when they need it. Priya's commute tests discretion in a crowd. Kavitha's tests how much the emergency assumes about the phone, the network and reading under pressure.
card: Urban commuter // Priya Sharma, 27 :: Marketing professional · Bengaluru :: _"I feel unsafe during late-night commutes. Current safety apps feel complicated, and I don't want a bulky or obvious device."_ :: **Frustrations.** Crowded or late trips feel unsafe. A visible device marks her as vulnerable. Menus cost time when she needs to act. :: **Goals.** A one-handed gesture. A ring she'd wear as jewellery. Choice of call, share or SOS. :: **For the design:** discretion has to be built into the form, not offered as a setting.
card: Rural commuter · Sets the floor // Kavitha Devi, 38 :: Farmer · Sullia, Karnataka :: _"I feel unsafe walking alone to the market and fields. Technology is confusing. I wish there was an easy way to call for help."_ :: **Frustrations.** Help may be far away. Coverage drops in and out. Apps assume English and confidence. :: **Goals.** A trigger found by touch. Setup in a language she reads. To know what still sends offline. :: **For the design:** anything needing data, English or multi-step navigation in an emergency will not work.
note: Why Kavitha sets the floor :: Priya fits the defaults most pattern libraries assume. Kavitha gets none of them. I treated her constraints as non-negotiable, and the ring trigger, offline fallback and language first all came from that. Each one made the product better for Priya too.
small: Both are composite personas built from published research.

# 06 / Design principles || One rule above the rest, and one for each research theme.
p: When two ideas competed, these decided it. Every decision in the next section traces back to one of them.
quote: The one rule above the rest :: Never reach for the phone.
card: From discretion // Look like jewellery, act like an alarm. :: Ring form, palm-facing panel, a Fake Call that looks native.
card: From accessibility // Design for Kavitha first. :: Language first, a trigger found by touch, no reading during an SOS.
card: From proportionate response // Match the response to the threat. :: The three-rung escalation ladder.
card: From readiness // Decide before you leave. :: Setup, ring settings and contacts chosen ahead of time.
card: From connectivity // Tell the truth about delivery. :: Contacts on the device, SMS fallback, delivery status.

# 07 / Key decisions and trade-offs || Four decisions, and what each one cost.
label: Decision 1 · Form factor · Never reach for the phone
h: Where should the SOS start?
p: I sketched bands, cuffs, rings, clasp bracelets and domes, and judged each by the physical act of asking for help.
diagram: sketches | Early sketches of bands, cuffs, rings, clasps and domes
small: Early sketches of bands, cuffs, rings, clasps and domes.
card: Ruled out // Phone only :: The phone is the thing she can't reach.
card: Ruled out // Pendant :: Easy to reach, but pulling it into place makes the gesture visible.
card: Ruled out // Bracelet :: Competes with a watch, and the wrist gesture is hard to hide.
card: Chosen // Ring :: The thumb reaches a palm-facing panel on the same hand: one-handed, eyes-free and invisible at once.
note: What the ring cost :: Hard limits on battery and radio range, so the ring only triggers and the phone does everything else.
label: Decision 2 · Product shape · Match the response to the threat
h: A ladder with three rungs, not a panic button.
p: Most of a bad commute sits between fine and emergency, and most safety products only design for the two ends.
diagram: d-ladder | A three-rung ladder: uneasy, Fake Call; worried, Tag Along; in danger, Ring SOS. The lower two live in the app and need the phone, the SOS starts on the ring with no screen
card: Why it was worth it :: A fake call is deniable, so it draws no attention. If an SOS fires later, her Tag Along match already knows where she is. At the top everything fires in parallel, so one failed step cannot block the rest.
card: What I gave up :: Simplicity. Three responses are more to learn than one button, and the lower rungs need the phone in hand. Tag Along also raises trust and safety questions about matching strangers that still need a review.
label: Decision 3 · The trigger · The one I'd defend hardest
h: A 2 second hold, then 5 silent seconds to cancel on the phone.
p: A ring bumps tables, catches on gloves and presses against bag straps all day. A one-press SOS would alarm her contacts and nearby users for nothing, and after two or three false alarms she would take it off.
diagram: d-trigger | What HalO does: the ring is pressed; if not held for 2 seconds the bump is ignored; if held, the phone buzzes once with 5 silent seconds to cancel; if ignored the SOS fires
card: Why the cancel lives on the phone :: If she meant it, she ignores the phone and the SOS goes out. If it was an accident, the phone is usually what is in front of her. A longer hold would filter more bumps, but adrenaline distorts time: two seconds can already feel like ten.
card: What I gave up :: About 7 seconds before the alert goes out. Whether 2 seconds holds up for someone genuinely scared is the first thing I would test before this concept earns a build.
label: Decision 4 · Onboarding · Design for Kavitha first
h: Language before anything else.
p: One extra tap for Priya. The whole product for Kavitha.
table: Step | The common pattern | HalO, under 3 minutes
tr: 1 | Welcome | Language
tr: 2 | Sign up, in English | Pair the ring, learn the hold
tr: 3 | Settings | Two contacts
tr: 4 | Language · locked | Home, listening
tr: Result | A locked door for Kavitha: she never reaches the setting. | Every word after the first screen is in a language she reads. The only typing is two phone numbers.
note: The trade-off :: One more screen before any value, and four languages to start. Copy is written plainly so it survives translation.

# 08 / The flows || One setup, one hub, five things she can do.
p: Setup happens once, calmly. After that, Home lets her jump straight to whichever response fits the moment.
diagram: d-flows | First run once: language, the promise, three responses, meet the ring, pair the ring, two contacts. Then Home, pick any rung: SOS, Fake Call, Tag Along, Route, and Ring plus contacts
phones3: s-language | **Language.** The first question, in each language's own script. ;; s-home | **Home.** Hold pad, route search and every rung on one screen. ;; s-cancel | **Silent cancel.** The countdown and the one way out. ;; s-sos | **SOS active.** Status only, readable at a glance. ;; s-fakecall | **Fake Call.** Looks like a real call, on purpose. ;; s-tagalong | **Tag Along.** Companion ETA, contact and an exit in view.
list: **No fixed order.** She can open Fake Call, Tag Along or SOS from Home without climbing through the lower rungs first.
list: **Tag Along keeps numbers private.** Calls and messages stay in the app, sharing with her own contacts is a separate yes or no, and she can end the session early.
list: **Settings stay out of the emergency.** Gestures, alarm volume, flashlight and contacts are all set ahead of time.
label: The ring, from the app
h: Set up once, checked at a glance, never touched mid-emergency.
phones3: s-ring | **Ring status.** Ready or not, in one line. ;; s-gestures | **Gestures.** Presses for light and sound, holds for the rest. ;; s-levels | **Levels.** Strength, brightness and volume.
list: **Status answers "is it ready?" first.** Connection, battery in days rather than percent alone, and whether the trigger is live sit above the ring itself.
list: **Pausing is one tap,** for when she is home and the ring will be knocked about. The review found a paused trigger still reads "listening", so a paused banner is the fix.
list: **The gesture matches the stakes.** Light and alarm are short presses because a mistake costs nothing. Anything hard to undo is a hold.
list: **The SOS hold defaults to 2 seconds,** adjustable from 1 to 3, and the screen explains the trade-off in one line: short enough for shaking hands, long enough that a bag strap never gets there.
list: **The taser row comes from early exploration.** The screen already says HalO ships with alarm and light only where it is restricted, and section 11 explains why I would cut it.
small: Two gestures can still share the same press count. The fix, blocking duplicates and naming the gesture already using it, is in the review findings.

# 09 / Visual design system || Dark, quiet, and only two colours, both signals.
p: Built on the Opal design system. Hierarchy comes from white at set opacities and weight, so colour is free to mean one thing each.
label: Colour
swatch: #040404 | Ground #040404 ;; #1d1d1d | Surfaces white 2 / 5 / 12% ;; #ffffff | Text white 100 / 80 / 50% ;; #1A8A6B | Safe #1A8A6B ;; #35C79A | Safe #35C79A ;; #E5484D | SOS #E5484D
list: **Green** means safe, live or nearby.
list: **Red** means SOS and nothing else, so it is never spent on decoration or errors.
label: Type
h: Hold the panel on the back.
p: Two seconds, with your hand still at your side.
list: System UI only, four weights.
list: Headlines bold, tracking −0.022em, line height 1.04, white fading to 70%.
list: Body 15 to 17px at 80% white, line height 1.6.
label: Components
list: One primary action per screen: a white pill, 54px tall.
list: Secondary actions use glass with a hairline border.
list: Elevation is a soft white glow, never a dark shadow. Radii 12 to 32px.
label: Motion and exceptions
list: Ease-out, 240 to 350ms. Forward slides left, back slides right.
list: SOS screens are takeovers that fade and scale in, so they never feel like the next page.
list: **Fake Call breaks the system on purpose.** It copies the native call screen, because a fake call only works if the person next to her believes it.
note: Accessibility held up in review :: Every tap target cleared 44px, secondary text cleared 5:1 contrast on the dark ground, and status always pairs text and icon with colour.

# 10 / Testing and iteration || I walked through the built prototype the way Priya would.
p: An expert review, not a study with participants. Half the tasks ran end to end. The other half showed me where the prototype still breaks its own promises.
diagram: d-review | Method: cognitive walkthrough of 8 tasks, Nielsen's 10 heuristics, and axe-core WCAG 2.1 AA on 14 screens. 4 of 8 tasks ran end to end; 10 issues by severity: 1 critical, 4 high, 4 medium, 1 low
table: Severity | What I found | Fix
tr: **Critical** | Copy promises delivery it can't guarantee. Setup says alerts arrive "with or without a signal", and the SOS screen says "there is nothing else you need to do". | "Saved on this phone. If there's no signal, HalO keeps trying and tells you." Add a "not delivered yet, retrying" state.
tr: **High** | "I'm safe" ends an SOS in one tap, so anyone holding her phone can cancel it. | Hold to confirm, an optional PIN, and a duress PIN that looks like a stand-down but keeps the alert live.
tr: **High** | A paused trigger still reads "Connected and listening". | A paused banner on Home and Ring, with auto-resume.
tr: **High** | Onboarding says "In danger: one press", contradicting the 2 second hold. | "Hold the back panel for two seconds."
tr: **High** | Tag Along's Message and Call look like buttons but are plain text, invisible to a screen reader. | Make them real, labelled buttons.
tr: Medium | Tag Along lists unverified strangers, and the fake caller and a companion share a name. | Show how each person was verified, let her choose, let her name the fake caller.
tr: Medium | Flashlight and alarm can be set to the same number of presses. | Block duplicates and name the gesture already using it.
tr: Medium | Every destination routes to MG Road. | Wire the destination to the route before any user test.
tr: Low | No confirmation after Cancel, and Continue sits partly below the fold at 390×844. | "Cancelled. No one was told." Keep Continue in view.
note: What held up :: The countdown made cancelling feel safe, per-contact status answered "did anyone get it?" without another screen, and setup can't finish without two contacts, so an SOS always has someone to reach.
label: Iteration · UX writing
h: Words for a reader whose hands are shaking.
quote: The rule :: Each line states one true thing that has already happened.
table: Moment | Before | After
tr: Countdown | "Sending your SOS." | "Your SOS goes out in 4 seconds. Nothing has been sent yet. Cancel now and no one is told." The headline and the countdown no longer disagree.
tr: Active alert | "Location sent" ticked the instant the SOS fired. | Sending, sent and opened are separate. "Amma opened it" shows only once she has it. No promise the network hasn't kept.
tr: All-clear | "Amma and Rahul have been told you are safe." | "The all-clear is on its way to Amma and Rahul." It hasn't landed yet.
tr: Route label | "Safest route" | "Better lit. 4 min more than the quickest route, busier, two places open late." A trade-off she can judge, not a promise.
note: The line I'd defend in a content review :: "Nothing has been sent yet. Cancel now and no one is told." The cancel window only calms her if the copy proves nothing has left the phone, so the mechanism and the sentence are one decision.
small: Every SOS string is written in the fewest words that survive translation into Kannada, Tamil and Hindi.

# 11 / Edge cases and ethics || Where it fails, and where it could do harm.
p: A safety product is judged by its worst moment. These are the ones I designed for, the two still open, and the parts I would cut or gate.
label: When something fails
rows: Ring pressed by accident | The 2 second hold filters bumps, and the 5 second silent cancel catches the rest.
rows: Mobile data drops | The SOS falls back to SMS with her last known location.
rows: No data and no SMS | The app says it could not send, and never implies someone was told.
rows: Ring not ready | Ring status shows connection, battery and listening first.
rows: Someone grabs her phone · **Open** | One tap on "I'm safe" ends the SOS. Fix: hold to confirm, a PIN and a duress PIN.
rows: Trigger paused at home · **Open** | It still reads "listening". Fix: a paused banner and auto-resume.
label: Where it could cause harm
rows: Shock deterrent · **Cut** | Restricted in most of India. The alarm and flash keep the core value.
rows: Tag Along matching · **Gated** | A match is also a target. Opt-in, identity-masked, session-only and rated, with legal and trust and safety sign-off first.
rows: Crowd-reported risk zones · **Gated** | Can encode bias, as Citizen showed. Verify reports and let them decay.
rows: "Safest route" · **Reworded** | A promise she can't rely on. Show the trade-off instead.
list: **The concept survives losing the deterrent.** That told me the real product is the trigger and alert chain, not the hardware.
list: **Contacts live on the device and the map is cached,** so no lookup has to succeed at the moment she triggers.

# 13 / Reflection || What this project changed about how I design.
label: What I learned
list: **Fear is a usability constraint, and it's measurable.** Simplifying a screen helps a calm user. Removing the screen is what helps a frightened one.
list: **The user furthest from my own experience made the best decisions.** Offline-first, SMS fallback and language first all came from starting with Kavitha.
list: **Make waiting visible.** A cancel window prevents false alarms and delays real ones, so its timing has to earn its place.
list: **Design for delivery.** Starting, leaving the phone and being received are three different events, and each needs its own status.
label: Honest limits
list: Grounded in published research, not interviews with the women I designed for.
list: The ring isn't built, so battery, range and haptics are unknown.
list: Alerts are simulated in the prototype.
label: Before this earns a build
list: Stress-test the 2 second hold with a test ring. Bar: every participant triggers it first try, uncoached.
list: Interview commuters in Bengaluru and Sullia, in their own languages.
list: Get legal and trust and safety sign-off on Tag Along.
