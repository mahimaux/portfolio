# 00 / Overview || Problem and solution
card: Problem :: Small driving schools keep lessons, fees, documents and vehicles in paper registers, Excel and WhatsApp. None of them update each other, so a clash, an unpaid fee or a missing document shows up only once it has already cost a lesson.
card: Solution :: Two front ends on one shared record. A map-first web dashboard answers “where is everyone, and what needs attention?” A today-first mobile app tells an instructor who is next and lets them start and log the lesson in a tap.

# 01 / Context and origin || It started with a register at the front desk.
p: I signed up to learn to ride. The school kept every student in a handwritten book.
p: One learner's record was already awkward to manage. I wanted to know what happened when the same school had to keep lessons, fees, instructor assignments and vehicle maintenance straight at once, across a full day of cars on the road.
p: So I went looking at how other schools did it, and ended up visiting nine of them.
label: Market signal
h: Most schools I visited still run on paper.
stat: 60% | Registers
stat: 40% | Excel
p: India's driving school market is fragmented and largely informal. Purpose-built software exists, but the schools I visited managed records on paper or in a general-purpose spreadsheet.
small: Share of the 9 schools by main record-keeping tool, as reported in my research notes. Full method in section 03.
label: Why the record matters
h: The register is a legal trail, not just admin.
p: A learner who trains at a school gets a Form 5 driving training certificate. The school fills it from its own register.

tr: Register entry | enrolment no.
tr: Vehicle class | 2W / 4W

# 02 / Problem framing || They asked for better organisation. The work was breaking between people.
card: What schools asked for // “Better organisation.” :: That points to a tidier register: one filing system, fewer versions of the same sheet.
card: What I saw // A live, multi-vehicle operation coordinated through a group chat. :: That needs a shared record that moves between the office and the car while lessons are running.
table: | Office | Car
tr: Who | Supervisor at a desk | Instructor on the road
tr: Tools | register · Excel · phone | WhatsApp · memory · paper
tr: Schedule change → | sent to the WhatsApp group | Instructor works from an older message
tr: ← Lesson outcome | Never reaches the register | remembered, written up later
small: Today a schedule change and a lesson outcome travel in opposite directions, through different tools, with nothing in the middle that both sides can read.
label: Problem statement
p: A schedule change could live in a message while the office worked from an older copy. A fee or a missing document could go unnoticed until it delayed a lesson. Each task was manageable on its own. The failures happened between them.
quote: How might we :: carry one lesson record from the office schedule to the instructor's next action and back, without adding admin to either side?
small: Out of scope for now: a student app, instructor payouts and rescheduling, and an AI copilot. They are on the roadmap in section 13.

# 04 / Synthesis and insights || Seven clusters, four insights, one through-line.
p: I grouped interview notes by recurring problem. Every cluster came up with both supervisors and instructors.
table: Affinity clusters · managers + instructors | Insight it feeds
tr: Scheduling issues | 1 · Failures happen at the handoff between desk and car
tr: Performance tracking | 2 · Supervisors need to monitor, not look things up
tr: Payments · Document collection · Safety compliance | 3 · Fees, documents and vehicle checks fail silently
tr: User experience · Technology adoption | 4 · Any new tool competes with teaching time
small: Affinity clusters on the left, the insight each one feeds on the right. Insight 1 is the one the rest of the design hangs on.
card: 1 // Failures happen at the handoff between desk and car. :: _“It's difficult to monitor student progress over time.”_ Instructor :: **Design implication.** One lesson record that both apps read and write. A change at the desk reaches the car, and a lesson outcome gets back to the office without a call.
card: 2 // Supervisors need to monitor, not look things up. :: _“I need reports to identify which instructors need training or which students are lagging.”_ Manager :: **Design implication.** The supervisor's home screen shows live status for every lesson at once. Records are one click away, not the starting point.
card: 3 // Fees, documents and vehicle checks fail silently. :: _“Students often delay payments, and we don't have a proper system to send reminders.”_ Manager :: **Design implication.** The system raises what's overdue, missing or due for service, so nobody has to remember to check.
card: 4 // Any new tool competes with teaching time. :: _“Learning a new tool feels daunting when I already have a busy schedule.”_ Instructor :: **Design implication.** Familiar patterns, no training to start, and logging that takes less effort than the paper it replaces.

# 05 / Users and contexts || Same school, same lesson, two very different working days.
p: Framed by context and jobs, not demographics. This is where the web and mobile split came from.
table: Dimension | Supervisor | Instructor
tr: Where | At a desk in the office | In a car, between lessons
tr: Device | Large screen | Phone, often one hand free
tr: Time horizon | The whole school, today | The next lesson
tr: Attention | Scans many lessons at once | Glances, then drives
tr: Their question | What changed across the school today? | Who am I teaching next, and where?
tr: Cost of a miss | A clash, an unpaid fee, a missing document | A wasted trip or a lesson with no context
card: Job to be done · Supervisor // When lessons are running across the city, I want to see where every lesson stands and what's overdue, so I can fix a problem before it costs a lesson. :: **Functional.** Keep schedules, payments, documents and vehicles reconciled :: **Emotional.** Feel ahead of problems instead of hearing about them last :: **Hired today.** Register, Excel, the WhatsApp group, phone calls
card: Job to be done · Instructor // When I start my day or my next lesson, I want to know who, when, where and what we covered last time, so I can teach instead of calling the office. :: **Functional.** An accurate schedule, the learner's history, a fast way to log the lesson :: **Emotional.** Go into each lesson prepared, without learning new software :: **Hired today.** A morning call, chat messages, memory, paper progress sheets
label: One lesson, both roles
h: The lesson crosses between them three times. Each crossing is where it breaks.
flow: Schedule · Office | register · WhatsApp. Handoff: arrives late or wrong
flow: Confirm · Car | morning call to office
flow: Prepare · Car | no lesson history
flow: Drive · Car | with the learner. Monitor from the office: calls, if anything. Handoff: no live view
flow: Log · Car | memory, paper later. Handoff: written up late, or lost
flow: Follow up · Office | fees, documents, Excel
small: The lesson's path today. Steps inside one lane mostly work. The crossings between office and car are where information arrives late, can't be seen, or gets lost.

# 06 / Design principles || Four rules, one for each insight.
card: 1 // One lesson, one record :: Both apps read and write the same lesson. Nothing is typed twice. :: **From.** Insight 1 · the handoff :: **Shows up as.** A schedule edit reaching Today
card: 2 // Status before records :: The supervisor sees what's happening now before opening anything. :: **From.** Insight 2 · monitoring :: **Shows up as.** The map-first dashboard
card: 3 // Let the system remember :: Overdue fees, missing documents and service dates come to you. :: **From.** Insight 3 · silent failures :: **Shows up as.** Reminders beside the map
card: 4 // One clear next step :: In the car, show today and the next action, within reach of one thumb. :: **From.** Insight 4 · teaching time :: **Shows up as.** Today screen, one-tap start

# 08 / Exploration and trade-offs || Where should a supervisor look first?
p: I took rough paper versions of three dashboards to supervisors. With each one, their first question was about location.
card: A · Set aside // List-first :: Familiar and quick to sort. Supervisors still asked where everyone was.
card: B · Set aside // Session cards :: Showed more per lesson. Location still took effort to piece together.
card: C · Chosen // Map-first :: Active vehicles in view, reminders alongside, details one click away.
label: What each choice cost
h: At the desk I traded simplicity for coverage. In the car I did the reverse.
table: Decision | Why | Gave up
tr: **Map-first dashboard** · Web | Location was the first question every time | A sortable overview; editing and history moved into detail views
tr: **One tap to begin a lesson** · Mobile | Learner, time and vehicle are already on screen | A confirm step. A false start also starts tracking
tr: **Location only during a lesson** · Both | Instructors need to trust what is recorded | A view of vehicles between lessons
tr: **Road-sign yellow and charcoal** · Both | Reads as driving at a glance | Colour can't carry status alone

# 10 / Design system and visual language || Road paint on tarmac.
p: Lime and charcoal come from road markings, the visual language both users already read every day. Number plates and times are set in mono, the way they appear on a plate or a dashboard.
swatch: #DFF20F | Lime · web #DFF20F ;; #D9F24B | Lime · mobile #D9F24B ;; #2E2F29 | Charcoal #2E2F29 ;; #0B0C08 | Ink #0B0C08 ;; #F5F6EF | Surface #F5F6EF ;; #858684 | Muted text #858684
label: Shared across both apps
p: Status pills, the lesson card, the primary button, initials avatars and mono numerals look the same on desk and phone. The layouts differ; the parts don't.
label: Two rules that keep it working
list: Lime is a fill, never text on white. Ink on lime is 15.7:1. Lime on white is 1.25:1.
list: Status never relies on colour alone. Every pill has a word, every pin a shape.
note: Audit finding :: The web and mobile limes drifted apart. Next step is one shared token.

# 13 / Reflection and next steps || What I'd carry into the next project.
card: Watch what people do, not just what they ask for. :: “Better organisation” and “running a school on WhatsApp” point to different products.
card: Density helps people who are monitoring. :: Hiding detail behind clicks slowed supervisors down. At the desk, showing everything at once was the right call.
card: Design both sides of the handoff. :: The office needs fees and progress. The instructor needs a fast way to update the lesson that feeds them.
