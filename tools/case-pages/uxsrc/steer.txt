# Backstory || I signed up to learn to ride.
p: The school wrote student records by hand. Updating a record was awkward. Finding an old one meant going back through the books. I wanted to see what happened when the same school had to keep track of lessons, fees, instructor assignments and vehicle maintenance.
h: Understanding the users
p: **Primary research.** I visited nine small to midsize driving schools and conducted ten semi-structured interviews with supervisors and instructors on site. I asked how they teach lessons, manage student and instructor records, and coordinate the work around each booking.

label: How schools keep records

label: Secondary research / Portfolio update
p: **Local context.** India’s Parivahan Form 5 ties a driving-school training certificate to a register entry, vehicle class and training dates. That shows why an enrolment record needs to be easy to retrieve.
small: Parivahan • Driving certificate, Form 5
p: **Product benchmark.** Drive Scout already combines scheduling, payments and records. For Steer, the specific design opportunity was carrying the same lesson from the office schedule to the instructor’s next action and back.
small: Drive Scout • Driving school management software

# Interviews and insights || Personas
p: Two composite personas capture where a manager and an instructor get stuck in the same workflow.
person: 04 | 32 / Manager, Swift Drive School // Monica Adams :: **Pain points.** Schedules and payments are hard to reconcile. Changes take too long to reach instructors and students. Documents are easy to misfile, and vehicle maintenance adds another record to keep current. :: **Needs.** A current schedule, a way to review student progress, and a shared place to coordinate changes and assignments. :: _What changed across the school today?_
person: 05 | 40 / Instructor, Ace Drivers Academy // Rajesh Nair :: **Pain points.** Schedules and cancellations arrive late. Paperwork takes time away from teaching. Progress is hard to track from one lesson to the next. :: **Needs.** Current assignments, a quick way to record attendance and performance, and a direct channel for coordinating with managers and students. :: _Who am I teaching next, and where?_

# Problem statement || Problem Statement
p: A schedule change could live in a message while the office worked from an older copy. A fee or a missing document could go unnoticed until it delayed a lesson. Each task was manageable on its own. The failures happened between them.
quote: How might we :: How might we create a unified digital solution that streamlines communication, scheduling, record management and daily operations so managers and instructors can spend more time on their core work and improve the experience for students?

# The concept || Digital Solutions
p: I designed two ways into the same system. Supervisors need to see the whole school at a glance. Instructors need to know what to do before the next lesson starts.
card: 01 / Web application :: **For managers and owners at a desk.** Track active lessons, maintain student and instructor records, monitor vehicles, and manage payments, documents and calendars.
card: 02 / Mobile application :: **For instructors between lessons.** Check the schedule, contact students, record attendance, see the route and log performance after class.
label: Ideation
h: Where should a supervisor look first?
card: List-first :: Familiar and quick to sort. Supervisors still asked where everyone was.
card: Session cards :: Showed more per lesson. Location still took effort to piece together.
card: Map-first :: Put active vehicles in view and kept lesson details within reach. I chose this direction.
p: I brought rough paper versions of all three to supervisors. Their first question was about location. I kept session status and reminders visible on the dashboard and put editing and history in detail views.
p: **On mobile, the trade-off reversed.** I chose one tap on Begin session from Session Details, where the instructor can already check the learner, time and vehicle. The prototype notes favour one tap. A false start still matters because it initiates tracking, so I’d measure start errors and recovery alongside speed.

# Key takeaways || Key Takeaways
p: The two interfaces changed how I think about the handoff between office work and field work.
label: 01 / What did I learn?
card: Design for both sides of the handoff. :: Fees and progress matter to the business. The instructor needs a quick way to update the lesson that feeds those records.
card: Start with the context of use. :: A supervisor can scan several sessions. An instructor in a parked car needs one clear next step.
card: Work with habits already in place. :: A pilot needs to account for how schools move from paper or Excel and how much training they can spare.
card: Research both roles. :: Talking to managers and instructors showed where information got stuck between them.
label: 02 / What’s next?
rows: Instructor copilot | I’m currently working on a copilot for the instructor app.
rows: Instructor salary payments | Explore pay calculations and payouts based on completed lessons.
rows: Instructor rescheduling | Let instructors move lessons within school-defined rules.
rows: Mobile lesson materials | Bring teaching guides and progress reports into the instructor app.
rows: Student app | Let learners track lessons, see feedback and pay fees.
rows: AI-powered insights | Explore recommendations for progress, schedules and vehicle maintenance.
note: What I’d test in a pilot :: Compare one paper-based and one Excel-based school with their own baselines. Measure time to locate an active session, morning calls, scheduling clashes, time to act on overdue items and instructor usage. Co-design tracking and test map labels and shapes before piloting.
